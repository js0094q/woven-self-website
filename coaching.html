1:1 Coaching

College experiences can leave you questioning your boundaries, your relationships, and your place in a community.

Individual coaching offers space to clarify your goals and choices as you navigate college life, coercive dynamics, and Greek-life experiences.

What we can focus on

  • Recognizing pressure and coercive patterns
  • Clarifying boundaries and practicing how to communicate them
  • Navigating belonging, loyalty, and decisions about Greek-life involvement
  • Identifying support and practical next steps

Coaching Disclaimer

Coaching is a non-clinical service and is separate from my psychotherapy practice. Coaching does not establish a therapist–client relationship and does not include mental health assessment, diagnosis, psychotherapy, or treatment of trauma or other mental health conditions. It is not a substitute for mental health treatment or crisis services. If your needs fall outside the scope of coaching, we will discuss appropriate referrals.

Beyond the Letters: Peer Support After Greek Life

A space for connection and shared perspective for people navigating difficult college and Greek-life experiences, including coercive dynamics, pressure to conform, unhealthy relationships, and questions about belonging.

Separate men’s and women’s groups. For adults 18+ who have left college and had fraternity or sorority involvement, including pledging without joining. Each group meets for six weekly, 75-minute sessions with six to eight participants. Cost: $25 per session ($150 total). Tentative start: November 2026, subject to sufficient enrollment; exact dates and times will be confirmed.

Discussions focus on recognizing patterns, exploring boundaries, rebuilding confidence in your own judgment, and identifying support. Participation does not require sharing your full story.

Peer support is a non-clinical service, not group therapy. It does not establish a therapist–client relationship or include mental health assessment, diagnosis, psychotherapy, or treatment. It is not a substitute for mental health treatment or crisis services. Participants are expected to respect one another’s privacy, but confidentiality among members cannot be guaranteed.
